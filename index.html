Welcome to Punjab

Punjab is famous for its rich history, beautiful religious places, traditional culture, festivals, delicious food and warm hospitality. Explore the ten famous tourist destinations of Punjab through this website.

1. Golden Temple

Location: Amritsar, Amritsar District, Punjab.

Historical Background: The Golden Temple is also known as Sri Harmandir Sahib. Its sacred pool was developed under Guru Ram Das Ji. Guru Arjan Dev Ji developed the shrine and installed the Adi Granth here in 1604. Maharaja Ranjit Singh later decorated the upper part of the shrine with gold.

Built / Established: Late 16th century; the shrine was completed in the early 17th century, with the Adi Granth installed in 1604.

Famous Tourist Attractions: Amrit Sarovar, Akal Takht, Central Sikh Museum, Clock Tower entrance and Heritage Street.

Speciality: Golden architecture, peaceful atmosphere and the free community kitchen known as Langar.

Famous Local Food: Amritsari Kulcha, Chole, Lassi and traditional Punjabi food.

2. Jallianwala Bagh

Location: Amritsar, Amritsar District, Punjab.

Historical Background: Jallianwala Bagh is an important historical memorial. On 13 April 1919, British troops under General Dyer opened fire on a peaceful gathering of people. The incident became a major event in India's freedom struggle.

Built / Established: The garden existed before 1919. The memorial was developed after India's independence. The National Memorial was inaugurated in 1961.

Famous Tourist Attractions: Martyrs' Memorial, Martyrs' Well, preserved bullet marks and the historical garden.

Speciality: It reminds visitors of the sacrifices made during India's freedom movement.

Famous Local Food: Amritsari Kulcha, Chole and Sweet Lassi.

3. Wagah-Attari Border

Location: Attari, Amritsar District, Punjab.

Historical Background: Wagah-Attari is the famous international border between India and Pakistan. The border became important after the Partition of India in 1947. It is known for the daily flag-lowering ceremony.

Built / Established: The modern international border dates from the Partition of India in 1947. The Beating Retreat ceremony developed as a border tradition afterwards.

Famous Tourist Attractions: Attari Border Gate, Beating Retreat ceremony, flag-lowering parade and visitor galleries.

Speciality: Patriotic celebrations, energetic marching and the ceremony performed by the Indian BSF and Pakistan Rangers.

Famous Local Food: Amritsari Kulcha, Chole Bhature and Lassi.

4. Gobindgarh Fort

Location: Amritsar, Amritsar District, Punjab.

Historical Background: Gobindgarh Fort is an important historical fort in Amritsar. It originated during the period of the Bhangi Misl and was later taken over and developed by Maharaja Ranjit Singh. It served as an important military stronghold.

Built / Established: 18th century origin. It was further developed during Maharaja Ranjit Singh's rule in the early 19th century.

Famous Tourist Attractions: Historic fort walls, Darbar Hall, Toshakhana, museums, cannons and cultural shows.

Speciality: Historic military architecture, royal heritage and displays of Punjab's history.

Famous Local Food: Amritsari Kulcha, Chole and Punjabi snacks.

5. Virasat-e-Khalsa

Location: Anandpur Sahib, Rupnagar District, Punjab.

Historical Background: Virasat-e-Khalsa is a famous museum dedicated to Sikh history and Punjabi heritage. It presents the history of Sikhism, the lives of Sikh Gurus and the formation of the Khalsa.

Built / Established: The museum was inaugurated in November 2011. Its construction and development took place over several years.

Famous Tourist Attractions: Museum galleries, historical paintings, multimedia displays, architectural bridges and Sikh heritage exhibitions.

Speciality: Beautiful modern architecture and an educational presentation of Sikh history.

Famous Local Food: Makki di Roti, Sarson da Saag and Punjabi Lassi.

6. Qila Mubarak

Location: Patiala City, Patiala District, Punjab.

Historical Background: Qila Mubarak is a famous royal fort and palace complex. It was founded by Baba Ala Singh, the founder of the Patiala state. It later became an important royal residence.

Built / Established: The foundation was laid in 1763 by Baba Ala Singh. Later rulers expanded and reconstructed parts of the fort.

Famous Tourist Attractions: Qila Androon, Darbar Hall, Ran Baas, Darshani Gate and royal museum collections.

Speciality: Royal architecture, historical courtyards, paintings and decorative interiors.

Famous Local Food: Patiala Shahi dishes, Punjabi Chole and Lassi.

7. Sheesh Mahal

Location: Patiala City, Patiala District, Punjab.

Historical Background: Sheesh Mahal is a beautiful royal palace associated with the rulers of Patiala. It was developed during the 19th century under Maharaja Narinder Singh. The palace reflects the royal lifestyle and artistic traditions of that period.

Built / Established: 19th century, during the reign of Maharaja Narinder Singh (1845–1862). An exact construction year is not established here.

Famous Tourist Attractions: Decorated palace interiors, mirror work, paintings, museum galleries, Banasar Tank and the suspension bridge.

Speciality: Beautiful mirror decorations, fresco paintings and royal architecture.

Famous Local Food: Patiala Shahi food, Punjabi sweets and Lassi.

8. Harike Wetland

Location: Harike, near the Beas-Sutlej confluence, in Tarn Taran and Ferozepur districts, Punjab.

Historical Background: Harike Wetland is one of the important natural wetland ecosystems of northern India. It developed around the Harike Barrage, where the Beas and Sutlej rivers meet. It is famous for migratory birds and biodiversity.

Built / Established: The wetland developed after the construction of Harike Barrage in 1952. It was designated a Ramsar site in 1990.

Famous Tourist Attractions: Harike Barrage, birdwatching areas, wetland landscapes and migratory bird habitats.

Speciality: Natural beauty, migratory birds, wildlife and ecological importance.

Famous Local Food: Traditional Punjabi food, Makki di Roti and Sarson da Saag in nearby areas.

9. Takht Sri Kesgarh Sahib

Location: Anandpur Sahib, Rupnagar District, Punjab.

Historical Background: Takht Sri Kesgarh Sahib is one of the five important Sikh Takhts. It is closely connected with Guru Gobind Singh Ji and the historic creation of the Khalsa Panth in 1699.

Built / Established: Its religious history is associated with the events of 1699. The present complex developed over time; an exact original construction date is not established here.

Famous Tourist Attractions: Main Gurdwara, sacred weapons associated with Sikh history, Anandpur Sahib heritage and the Hola Mohalla celebrations.

Speciality: Great religious importance, Sikh history and the annual Hola Mohalla festival.

Famous Local Food: Langar, Punjabi dishes, Makki di Roti and Sarson da Saag.

10. Pushpa Gujral Science City

Location: Jalandhar-Kapurthala Road, Kapurthala District, Punjab.

Historical Background: Pushpa Gujral Science City is an educational and entertainment centre developed to promote science and technology. It encourages students and visitors to learn through interactive exhibitions and scientific activities.

Built / Established: The foundation stone was laid on 17 October 1997. The first phase was inaugurated and opened to the public on 19 March 2005.

Famous Tourist Attractions: Digital Planetarium, IMAX Theatre, Dinosaur Park, Science Galleries, Robotics, 3D Theatre and interactive exhibitions.

Speciality: Science education, technology, space displays and exciting learning activities.

Famous Local Food: Kapurthala and Punjabi regional food, Amritsari Kulcha and Lassi.
